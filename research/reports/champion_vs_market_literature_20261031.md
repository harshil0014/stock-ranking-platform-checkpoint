# Champion versus alternatives: Indian equity literature and benchmark design

Research date: 31 October 2026. Scope: NSE and BSE equities, with ISIN deduplication across exchanges. This review supports the preregistered `champion_vs_market_v1` experiment; it does not establish a universal market leader.

## What constitutes a benchmark

There is no single public NSE/BSE stock-ranking accuracy leaderboard used by every commercial platform. A credible comparison needs three separate questions: can a model order future stock outcomes on matched stocks and dates; can its selected portfolio improve investor cashflows and risk; and can another service supply genuinely historical scores for the same test? An index answers the investment question, not the stock-pair question. Advertised accuracy percentages, a platform's rating scale, and a fund CAGR are different quantities.

The practical investment baseline is a broad total-return index, with Nifty 500 TRI as the primary broad Indian comparator and Nifty 50, Midcap 150 and Smallcap 250 as size controls. Momentum, quality, value, low-volatility and multifactor TRI series ask whether an elaborate ranker earns its complexity against systematic alternatives. BSE 500 price returns are a secondary, explicitly dividend-excluding series until BSE 500 TRI is available. An index is a theoretical portfolio; applying trading-cost scenarios does not turn it into the realised NAV of an investable index fund.

Official sources: [NSE historical data](https://www.niftyindices.com/reports/historical-data), [Nifty 500](https://www.niftyindices.com/indices/equity/broad-based-indices/NIFTY-500), [BSE 500](https://www.bseindices.com/indices-details/code/17). Machine-readable acquisition receipts record returned index identities and response hashes. HTTP 200 alone is not accepted as data: the old NSE endpoint returned an HTML page, and the public BSE TRI ticker returned an empty response.

Launch dates matter. Midcap 150 and Smallcap 250 launched in April 2016; Nifty500 Quality and Low Volatility launched in December 2024; the multifactor index launched in February 2025. Their earlier series are publisher backtests, not observations of a live published strategy. The scorecard separates launch-era history from backfilled history. Sources: [2016 NSE announcement](https://www.niftyindices.com/Press_Release/ind_prs22022016_1.pdf), [quality factsheet](https://www.niftyindices.com/Factsheet/Factsheet_Nifty500Quality50.pdf), [low-volatility factsheet](https://www.niftyindices.com/Factsheet/Factsheet_Nifty500LowVolatility50.pdf), [multifactor factsheet](https://www.niftyindices.com/Factsheet/Factsheet_Nifty500MultifactorMQVLv50.pdf).

## What transfers from foreign markets

**Price factors are the first hurdle.** Jegadeesh–Titman momentum, George–Hwang 52-week highs, Gervais–Kaniel–Mingelgrin volume effects, and volatility research motivate transparent alternatives. The test includes medium-term momentum, risk-adjusted momentum, high proximity and recency, reversal, trend efficiency, downside and volatility ranks, and simple combinations. These are adaptations using this project's available price features, not exact replications of the original portfolios or of NSE's official weighting rules. US long–short results do not imply a profitable Indian long-only SIP strategy.

**Fundamental factors require historical accounting availability.** Fama–French, gross profitability and Quality Minus Junk motivate quality, value and profitability comparisons. The present stock-level panel lacks a certified point-in-time accounting history; today's ratios cannot be substituted into old dates. Actual published quality/value TRI histories provide investment alternatives, but they do not test whether our model beats a stock-level fundamental ranker on matched securities. Current filing timestamps being captured prospectively will help future tests, without repairing past publication dates.

**India already has an important factor reference.** Agarwalla, Jacob and Varma's [IIM Ahmedabad factor library](https://faculty.iima.ac.in/iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/) supplies Indian market, SMB, HML, WML and RF series. Its current release covers October 1993–December 2025, uses NSE/BSE data, excludes very small and penny stocks, and can revise earlier values when the underlying database changes. The downloadable survivorship-adjusted daily file was acquired with a hash. This is useful for exposure attribution; it does not independently validate this project's surviving-stock panel or certify positive alpha. [Original working paper](https://faculty.iima.ac.in/iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/four-factors-India-90s-onwards-IIM-WP-Version.pdf).

**Strong tabular models deserve equal treatment.** Gu, Kelly and Xiu's empirical asset-pricing work motivates nonlinear interactions while also reminding us that momentum, liquidity and volatility remain useful baselines. Ridge, elastic net, histogram boosting, Extra Trees, random forest, LightGBM regression and LambdaRank, CatBoost YetiRank and XGBoost NDCG are trained with an identical deterministic whole-query cap and annual mature-label cutoff. Both return and balanced-risk objectives are tested. This is a controlled fixed-budget comparison, not each algorithm's maximum achievable accuracy after unlimited tuning. [GKX](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhaa009), [LightGBM](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/6449f44a102fde848669bdd9eb6b76fa-Abstract.html).

**Sequence and relationship models are plausible, not automatic upgrades.** Qlib provides licensed implementations of GRU, attention LSTM, Transformer, GAT and HIST. MASTER conditions stock interactions on market context. We retain their pinned neural class definitions, train from scratch, and adapt inputs to Indian OHLCV, investor parameters and same-date market context. Eight recent sessions come from a past 252-session normalized input window. The 512-issuer sequence cohort is explicitly smaller than the full stock panel. Same-input Ridge and LightGBM controls help distinguish architecture effects from different features. HIST uses current retrospective sectors, not historical concept memberships; graph models retain unlabeled feature-available stocks as context and mask only their training losses. Sources: [Qlib](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11189), [HIST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13716), [MASTER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15235).

**Drift adaptation needs more mature dates.** DoubleAdapt and TRA are reviewed as follow-up candidates. Their appeal is adaptation under changing distributions and multiple patterns. A small number of overlapping 12-month SIP outcome dates does not support casually copying daily-return online-learning claims. They are not labelled tested or failed in this round. [DoubleAdapt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09862), [TRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.12950).

The executed source inspection supports the following narrower interpretation of each architecture:

| Architecture | Mechanism retained from pinned code | Indian adaptation and important limit |
|---|---|---|
| GRU | Gated recurrent state over each stock's feature sequence | Ten input columns, two32-unit recurrent layers, final scalar forecast; no cross-stock information |
| ALSTM | Recurrent representation with attention pooling over time | Same bounded Indian sequence representation; attention weights are not causal explanations |
| Transformer | Positional encoding and temporal self-attention | Four heads and two32-dimensional layers; eight sessions are much shorter than the full252-session normalization window |
| GAT | Learned stock-to-stock attention after temporal encoding | Full same-date/profile feature cohort; learned similarity rather than a verified supply-chain graph |
| HIST | Predefined concept sharing, hidden-concept sharing and individual residual information | Current-sector one-hot concepts replace the original concept source; both concept provenance and shorter inputs limit generalisation |
| MASTER | Market-conditioned softmax feature gate, temporal attention, spatial attention and final temporal pooling | Twelve input-only cross-stock context statistics replace original market features; ten stock/profile inputs,32-dimensional model and four temporal/spatial heads |

The original Qlib/MASTER training wrappers, data providers, validation processors and pretrained weights are not imported. The adapter extracts the named neural class definitions without rewriting their internals, then supplies its own explicit mature-label training loop. This avoids suggesting that importing a repository also reproduces its published preprocessing, dataset and experimental protocol. Losses are masked for unknown outcomes while all feature-available stocks remain in graph context. The matched linear/tree controls isolate the short sequence representation more directly than the full-feature champion comparison does.

**Foundation-model results require contamination checks.** Kronos models financial candles, while TimesFM, Chronos and Moirai target broader time-series forecasting. PatchTST, TimesNet and iTransformer offer alternative temporal structures. TabPFN and TabICL address tabular prediction, with different training-size and task constraints; TabM offers efficient neural ensembling. These families are reviewed, not all executed here. The previous actual Kronos feature pilot remains separate evidence; its pretrained corpus/timing prevents treating old financial test dates as an unquestioned clean holdout. A pretrained model's published percentage improvement does not transfer to Indian SIP labels. See the registry for individual primary references.

**A portfolio optimiser is a different product.** AlphaStock, deep asset-pricing and autoencoder work can motivate objectives and representations, but require different datasets or trading assumptions. Long–short leverage, continuous rebalancing and a Sharpe optimisation target cannot silently replace this product's long-only stock ranking and recurring investment problem. Equal allocation remains a transparent portfolio control. Execution research motivates costs, liquidity and capacity analysis; fixed basis-point scenarios here are sensitivity tests, not calibrated Indian market-impact estimates.

**Text and agent systems need timestamped information, not present-day summaries.** FinBERT supplies a financial sentiment representation; FinGPT supplies financial-language-model methods. StockNet combines historical text and price, CMIN combines text and stock relationships, LLMFactor extracts news factors, and FinAgent combines market modalities and tool use. These expand the research beyond candle-only forecasting, but sentiment classification accuracy is not stock-return ranking accuracy. None is represented as executed here: a timestamped Indian news/filing corpus, issuer mapping, publication delays, model-weight cutoff and pretraining contamination audit are needed first. Feeding current filings or a current LLM's remembered company history into a 2020 prediction would invalidate the comparison. Sources: [FinBERT](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10063), [FinGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06031), [StockNet](https://aclanthology.org/P18-1183/), [CMIN](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.679/), [LLMFactor](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.185/), [FinAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18485).

## How the comparison avoids easy wins

Annual training excludes every outcome whose endpoint is not strictly before January 1 of the prediction year. Hyperparameters and seeds are fixed before new performance tables. Stochastic algorithms use three seeds; deterministic fits are counted once. Development selection is persisted before exposed-period metric computation. The 2024–2025 period was already inspected in prior rounds and is called exposed, never fresh.

Rank IC, exact tie-aware pair accuracy, NDCG at 3/10/20, goal frequency, selected-stock XIRR, target shortfall, downside, cross-asset tails and selection churn answer different questions. A selected stock's drawdown is not portfolio drawdown. Separate daily cashflow simulations evaluate the latter, with zero/10/25/50 basis-point buy/sell scenarios. Portfolio selections use full feature-available rows; missing future labels do not improve the selection universe. A missing selected history or unresolved corporate action invalidates the whole portfolio observation rather than silently removing a loser.

White's Reality Check, the factor-zoo literature, backtest-overfitting research and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio motivate accounting for the whole candidate search. This round reports paired temporal-block uncertainty across all compared policies; neither 70 related model columns nor overlapping 12-month outcomes constitute 70 independent discoveries. The original 0.0101 IC and 0.63 percentage-point attainment hurdles are incremental selection-noise guides, not absolute acceptable-accuracy levels. A new familywise hurdle is estimated for this broader family.

Probability quality is measured using Brier, log loss and reliability error against past-mature climatology. A raw rank percentile is not a probability. Calibration fits use only earlier out-of-time predictions whose outcomes have matured by the current decision date. Later exposed dates may use earlier exposed outcomes once matured under this fixed prequential rule; no exposed optimisation is performed. A declining market hit rate can make relative ordering useful while absolute probability calibration fails.

## Remaining evidence limits

This round cannot establish a survivorship-free market leadership claim. Historical membership and terminal-price reconstruction remain incomplete, sectors are retrospective, financial point-in-time features are incomplete, and the frozen live runtime has not yet produced an eligible fresh holdout. Provider-adjusted stock prices are not exchange-certified total-return paths. Correlated overlapping cohorts limit inference. A poor foreign-model adaptation does not refute its original paper; an unavailable commercial history is untested rather than a loss. The accompanying scorecard must report these statuses alongside every apparent win.
