## 2. Earlier models, including Baseline

Recomputed sector comparisons against **33 retained earlier policies** across 81 opponent/period combinations. **59 of 81** have all six aggregate Top 3 metrics nonworse; 22 do not. Metrics are median constituent XIRR, mean constituent drawdown, achievement, shortfall, NDCG and sector rank IC. This is a period-average test, not dominance for every stock, date or profile.

Against original V17, all six period averages improve in all three periods. Return improvements are +0.37, +1.00 and +3.06 percentage points respectively; drawdown improvements are +4.42, +3.09 and +2.09 points. Counterexamples remain: Original Personalized has about 0.39 points higher development return; V14 has about 0.96 points better recent mean drawdown.

A separate **Baseline four-factor formula replay** uses observed 1Y, 3Y, 5Y growth and ATH-age competition ranks within sectors, requiring five years of available history. This is a strict common-feature replay under today's sector mapping, not a reproduction of the original archived point-in-time Baseline product cohort. Current Top 3 development return is 35.42% versus Baseline replay 37.70%, with better current drawdown (−26.38% versus −31.89%). Current early/recent return improves, but recent achievement is lower. Baseline is not uniformly beaten.

Strict Research V2's historical fundamental eligibility and point-in-time industry assignments remain outside this matched panel. Its superiority or inferiority is not established. Archived partial-coverage ML strategies retain their smaller samples; no universal league table is inferred from incomparable populations.

## 4. Research and experiments after the benchmark

Primary research considered: [AQR Quality Minus Junk](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Working-Paper/Quality-Minus-Junk), [George/Hwang on 52-week highs](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/tgeorge/papers/gh4-paper.pdf), and [Residual Momentum](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927539811000041). These motivate hypotheses; they do not validate our Indian-stock weights. Current financial statements were not backfilled into past dates as if known then. Risk-adjusted momentum is a volatility-scaled proxy, not regression residual momentum.

| Round | Configurations | Test | Outcome |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Additional signals/local weights | 60 | Sustained high support, annual-high proximity, six-month momentum, risk-adjusted momentum, professor score, tail risk, combined strength; local high/risk weights | No complete promotion-gate pass with positive gain. Exact incumbent control ties are not improvements. |
| Chronological learned challenger | 16 blends | Four shallow LightGBM models, four blend strengths; yearly predictions trained only on outcomes matured before each year's first test date | No strict gate pass. Training cutoffs and model files retained. |
| Targeted interactions | 48 | Momentum/annual-high signals combined with explicit risk offsets | No strict gate pass. |

Total **124 additional configurations**. Development-only gates required nonworseness across six aggregate metrics at Top 1/3/5, positive utility and, for deterministic rounds, profile/sector safeguards. The learned round failed aggregate gates before later safeguards were relevant.

### Promising near-miss, investigated rather than discarded silently

`balanced_strength_0.2` mixes 80% current score with 20% equal parts annual-high proximity, sustained high support, six-month momentum and historical goal percentile. All 18 development aggregate differences were positive, but only **54.90%** of sectors had nonnegative utility, below the locked **55%** gate. It was therefore evaluated separately as a diagnostic, not reclassified as a winner.

Its Top 3 return averages improve in all periods: 3.08%→3.42%, 35.05%→35.21%, and 0.31%→1.43%. However, recent Top 1 return declines **26.82%→15.08%**, and recent Top 5 mean drawdown worsens slightly. It is a documented shortlist tradeoff, not a clean superior replacement. Promotion would require a consciously changed objective and fresh validation, not pretending the original gate passed.

The strongest six-month-momentum development return trial also worsened drawdown and shortfall. More return alone did not trigger promotion.

## 5. Verification, scope and stopping point

- Workbook hash and formulas rechecked; source unchanged.
- All 33 opponent policies have paired sector evidence; original artifact provenance is preserved.
- Corrected serving exports cover nine profiles. EMROCK is excluded from sector ranks in each; default eligibility is 1,746.
- Five targeted tests passed for peer-score isolation, arithmetic, small/unknown-group behavior and the classification correction/cache.
- Desktop/mobile sector browser checks passed with no JavaScript errors. Local service restarted with the reviewed classification overlay.
- The preceding 48-candidate tuning round and its eight tests remain documented separately; they are not counted as new trials here.

The historical comparisons evaluate score policies with current sector membership and retained price histories. They inherit survivorship, historical revision, repeated-research and execution limitations. The new current classification correction is **not** backfilled into the historical benchmark or claimed as an investment-performance gain. No significance or fresh holdout claim is made. “Mean constituent drawdown” is not portfolio maximum drawdown; averaged constituent XIRRs are not portfolio XIRRs.

This sequence stops at the prespecified finite search boundary. Continued arbitrary search on these same exposed dates would not prove absolute optimality. The remaining high-value work needs new evidence: historical sector memberships, dated sector-specific fundamentals, verified lot/liquidity assumptions and prospective frozen rankings. The current weights are retained because the tested replacements did not meet the agreed clean-improvement objective, not because all possible models have been exhausted.
